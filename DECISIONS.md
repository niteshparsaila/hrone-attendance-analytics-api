# Design decisions

1. **Indexes.** A unique `emp_code` index prevents duplicate employees. A unique `(emp_code, date)` index guarantees one attendance record per employee per attendance day and resolves simultaneous punch-ins safely. A `(date, emp_code)` index supports the attendance list's date ordering; additional department/join-date and date/status indexes support common filters and reporting. I would avoid adding indexes without checking query plans because every index adds write and storage cost.
2. **Punch-in race.** Both requests may check the employee, but only one insert can satisfy the unique `(emp_code, date)` index. MongoDB accepts one; the other gets a duplicate-key error translated to HTTP 409. No check-then-insert assumption is used for uniqueness.
3. **Ties.** The leaderboard uses MongoDB `$rank` after sorting by total late minutes. Employees tied at the cutoff share a rank, and every row whose rank is at most `limit` is returned. The next rank is skipped, as in standard competition ranking.
4. **Headcount.** Department summary starts from eligible employees and looks up attendance records. Preserving employees with no matching logs ensures they remain in the department headcount; employees joining after month-end are excluded.
5. **100x data.** I would inspect real execution plans and workload, then tune compound indexes and consider pre-aggregated monthly reporting if latency justified the added write/update complexity. I would also add integration tests against a production-like MongoDB version and measure the expensive lookup pipelines.
